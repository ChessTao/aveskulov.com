Students

Over the years, I have worked with chess players from every continent except Antarctica. Some students came with the goal of earning titles or norms, while others wanted to improve their tournament results, stabilise their play, or make their training more professional. Here are selected student achievements collected from the earlier versions of this website and updated with the information available in this project.

Felix Tulchinsky

Felix reached the International Master title and won the Israeli U18 Championship in 2026, finishing first on tiebreak after scoring 7/9.

Miras Assylov

Miras won a medal at the World Youth Rapid Championship and bronze at the 2024 World Youth Blitz Championship O16. At the 2025 Ljubljana Open he scored a shared-winning 7/9 and finished on the podium after tiebreaks. He then reached the 2400 rating mark and became one of Kazakhstan's strongest young players.

Miro Ok

Miro placed 4th in the Bavarian Championship MeisterKlasse. In 2025 he won the Chessence Open B Group and the JEF Belgian Youth Rapid Circuit U10 season standings. In 2026 he became Belgian U10 National Champion.

Bogdan Bilovil

Bogdan finished second in the 2023 German U18 Championship and again took second place in the 2024 German U18 Championship. At the 2024/2025 Rilton Cup he scored 6.5/9, and in 2025 he earned his first GM norm at the Hotel Stockholm North GM tournament.

Svyatoslav Bazakutsa

Svyatoslav won the 2022 European U14 Championship with 8/9 from the 22nd seed, gaining 165 Elo points. He added his first IM norm at the 2023 European Individual Championship and his first GM norm by winning the 2024 Norway Chess Open.

Michael Blair

Over two years, Michael improved from CFC 1600 to a peak of 2139, from FIDE 1600 to 1892, and from Chess.com 1900 to 2400 in blitz and rapid. He earned the Canadian National Candidate Master title by crossing 2000 and scoring three 2100 performance norms.

Tom O'Gorman

Tom won the 2020 Irish Championship.

Arnav Srivastava

Arnav produced his best tournament performance at the 2018 British Championship, taking second place in the Under-12 section.

Dmitriy Kushko

Dmitriy shared first place at the 2018 Myrhorod Open. At 14 he earned the National Master title, placed third in the Ukrainian U20 Championship, and became Ukrainian U20 Blitz Champion.

Viktor Korennoi

Viktor gained 80 rating points in about half a year after strong performances at the 2017 Chigorin Memorial and the 2018 Moscow Open, crossing 2300 and earning the FIDE Master title.

Justin Wang

Justin won bronze in the Open U10 section of the 2015 World Youth Championship, also recorded in the FIDE winners report. By age 13 he had completed the requirements for the International Master title, securing all three norms before the final round of his events.

Kyle Wang

Kyle was the 2018 Texas State Grade Championship 1st Grade Co-Champion, the 2019 Texas State Grade Championship 2nd Grade Champion, and the 2019 National Elementary K-5 Championship K-1 Co-Champion. In 2023 he won the 2023 National K-12 Grade Championship, 6th Grade section, and in 2024 he tied for third place at the National Middle School K-8 Championship.

Francois Oberholzer

Francois improved his South African rating from 1756 to 1954 in less than a year. He placed 3rd in the National Championship U16 in 2010, won the RSA U18 Championship in 2011, finished second in the 2012 African Youth Championship, became a FIDE Master, and placed clear fourth in the 2014 South African Open from the 23rd seed. His later 2015 South African Open result is also recorded by FIDE.

Sergei Aslanov

Sergei gained 106 Elo points over roughly three months, rising from 2226 to 2300+ and continuing to improve afterwards.

Dr. Jeffrey Plew

Jeffrey improved steadily through three Doerberl Cups, defeated his first master in the 2012 Doerberl Cup, scored a performance around 2200 at the World Open, and won both the NSW Country Championship and the Dubbo Open in 2013.

Epiphany Peters

Epiphany won the 2012 Japan Women's Championship.

Roberto de Abreu

Roberto earned the FIDE Master title after sharing first-second place in the U16 section of the 2012 African Championship.

Bhaskar Gupta

Bhaskar made rapid progress after beginning serious work in September 2011, improving from 1933 to 2195 in less than one year.
