About Valeriy Aveskulov

Chess Career

I was born in 1986 in Ukraine and grew up in the Luhansk region. My father introduced me to chess when I was six years old. I received the International Master title in 2002 and became a Grandmaster in 2006. Around that time, I understood that chess had become not only my passion, but also my profession.

Among my most important results are becoming the Ukrainian Champion in 2007, winning three medals at the first World Mind Sports Games in Beijing in 2008, and finishing second in the World Youth Rapid Championship under 12 in Paris in 1998. I also won the 2007 OCF North American FIDE Open in Stillwater and Kazakhstan Hopes 2005 in Pavlodar. As a member of the Law Academy chess team, I became a multiple Ukrainian club champion, and I have also been a prize-winner in other international tournaments.

Coaching Experience

I have been coaching online since 2009. During this time, I have worked with players from different countries and levels. At the moment, most of my students are rated between 2100 and 2500, and my work is mainly focused on ambitious players who want a more serious and structured training process.

My coaching work includes individual lessons, training plans, online training camps, and game preparation. More about my approach can be found on the Method page, while student progress and achievements are collected on the Students page.

Author and Course Creator

Besides coaching, I am also an author and course creator. My book Attack with Black was published by Gambit Publications, and I have created courses and articles for platforms such as Chessable and Modern Chess. You can find links to my main works on the Publications page.

Life Beyond the Board

Today, I live in Budapest with my wife and four children. Coaching is my main work, but I still play from time to time and occasionally test my ideas in grandmaster round-robin tournaments in Budapest. You can also explore a selection of my best games.

I hold a Candidate of Legal Sciences degree; my dissertation was devoted to the legal regulation of annual leave .
